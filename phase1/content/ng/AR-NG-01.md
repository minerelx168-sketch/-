# NCC-DMS Registration Guide: Step-by-Step for Nigeria 2026

When the Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) switched on its new Device Management System (NCC-DMS) in October 2025, it changed how every phone connects to MTN Nigeria, Glo, Airtel and 9mobile. The system is a Central Equipment Identity Register (CEIR)-style platform that holds the IMEI of every device that touches a Nigerian SIM. If your IMEI is not in the register, or if it has been flagged as stolen or unapproved, your phone risks losing service as enforcement tightens through 2026.

This is the registration walkthrough most Nigerian buyers actually need. It is short, practical, and follows the route the NCC and your network use today. If you only have five minutes, jump to the four-step portal section.

## What NCC-DMS Actually Is

NCC-DMS is the operational platform behind the Type Approval Business Rule 2024 and the Business Rules for Communications Subscribers Regulations 2025. In plain English, it is one national database that the four major mobile network operators (MNOs) sync with daily.

It does three things:

1. Confirms whether a device's IMEI is type-approved for use in Nigeria.
2. Cross-checks the IMEI against stolen, cloned and counterfeit registers shared between NCC, the Nigerian Customs Service, SON (Standards Organisation of Nigeria), NITDA and NIMC.
3. Tells the MNOs whether to allow that IMEI to carry voice, SMS or data on their network.

Enforcement is gradual. Phones already in active use before October 2025 are being onboarded automatically through the MNO sync. Newly imported or newly resold phones are the ones most likely to need manual action. You can confirm your status right now with a [Free IMEI Check](/ng/) before you go any further.

## Who Must Register

You should register, or at least verify your IMEI on NCC-DMS, if any of the following is true:

- You bought a phone outside Nigeria and brought it in after October 2025.
- You bought a used phone from Computer Village in Lagos, Alaba International, or any informal market.
- You imported a phone through a courier or as personal luggage.
- Your phone shows "no service" on a known-good SIM even when in a coverage area.
- The seller could not produce a Nigerian retail receipt or a NCC type-approval reference.

Phones bought new from authorised dealers (for example, MTN stores, Slot, Pointek, Jumia official) are normally registered automatically by the importer. You do not need to do anything for those, but a verification check is still wise.

## Step-by-Step: Registering on the NCC-DMS Portal

The NCC-DMS public portal is the channel for individual device verification. Follow these four steps from your mobile browser. The flow is mobile-first and works on 3G if you are patient.

**Step 1 — Find your IMEI.** Dial `*#06#` on the phone you want to register. A 15-digit number appears. Dual-SIM phones show two IMEIs; you will use both. Write them down or screenshot them. If the phone is dead, the IMEI is also printed on the original box and inside the SIM tray on most modern devices.

**Step 2 — Open the NCC-DMS verification page.** Visit the public NCC-DMS check page from the official NCC website (`ncc.gov.ng`). Avoid links shared on WhatsApp groups — fake NCC pages are already circulating. Enter your IMEI and your NIN (National Identification Number). The NIN-SIM linkage rules from 2024 mean your IMEI is tied to the same NIN as your SIM.

**Step 3 — Submit supporting proof if prompted.** If the portal returns "Device requires verification", upload the requested documents. The common set is: a clear photo of the IMEI label on the box, the purchase receipt, and for imported phones, the Customs declaration form. Files must be under 2MB each — compress before upload to save data.

**Step 4 — Wait for the confirmation SMS.** Successful registrations return an SMS to the SIM linked to your NIN within 24 to 72 hours. The message confirms the IMEI is now on the active register and shows a reference number. Keep that reference; you will need it if you ever change networks or sell the device.

## Fees and Realistic Timelines

There is no fee for verifying or registering a phone you already own. The NCC has stated registration of personal devices is free at the point of use. Watch out for "agents" at Computer Village charging ₦5,000 to ₦15,000 to do this for you — the portal is self-service.

What can cost money is bringing in an unregistered import. If your phone was not declared at Customs, you may still owe import duty before SON and NCC will release the IMEI. That figure depends on the device value and falls under Customs jurisdiction, not NCC.

Timeline expectations from the first weeks of enforcement:

- Brand-new phone from an authorised dealer: instant, already on the register.
- Used phone with full paperwork: 24–72 hours after portal submission.
- Imported phone needing Customs cross-check: 5–10 working days.
- Phones flagged as suspect (stolen, cloned, no type approval): can take several weeks and may require a Lagos or Abuja office visit.

## Next Steps After You Register

Once you receive the confirmation SMS, do three things to protect yourself.

First, save your IMEI, the NCC reference number, and a photo of the receipt in your email — not just on the phone itself. If the phone is later stolen, you will need those to report and block it.

Second, if you have more than one phone, repeat the process for each IMEI. A dual-SIM device counts as two IMEIs and must be registered as one device.

Third, if you plan to sell the phone in future, transfer the registration to the buyer through the same portal. Selling a registered phone without transferring it leaves the IMEI tied to your NIN, which becomes a problem if the buyer misuses the device.

NCC-DMS is the new floor for mobile use in Nigeria. Treat it like NIN registration: a one-time task that prevents months of headache later.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is NCC-DMS registration compulsory for all phones in Nigeria?**
Yes. Under the Business Rules for Communications Subscribers Regulations 2025, every IMEI that connects to a Nigerian network must be on the NCC-DMS register. Enforcement is being rolled out gradually through 2026, but new and imported devices are already affected.

**How much does NCC-DMS registration cost?**
Verifying or registering a personal device is free on the official NCC portal. Any "agent" asking for ₦5,000 or more is not authorised by NCC. You may separately owe Customs import duty if the phone entered Nigeria undeclared.

**Can I register a phone bought from Computer Village?**
Yes, but only if you can produce either a receipt or original box. Phones with scratched-off IMEI labels or no proof of purchase risk being flagged as stolen and may not be registrable.

**What happens if I do not register my IMEI?**
Through 2025 and early 2026 the system is in soft-enforcement mode. As the rollout advances, unregistered IMEIs will lose voice, SMS and data service on MTN, Glo, Airtel and 9mobile. Existing actively-used phones are being onboarded automatically; new and imported devices are not.

**Does NCC-DMS replace the NIN-SIM linkage?**
No. NIN-SIM links your identity to your SIM card. NCC-DMS links your IMEI to the network. Both are required, and the two systems cross-reference each other.

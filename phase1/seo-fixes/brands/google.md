# Google IMEI Check — Free Lookup for Google Pixel Devices

Google's Pixel line is the reference implementation of Android — first to receive OS updates, first to run new AI features like Magic Editor, Circle to Search, and on-device Gemini Nano. Global market share is modest (roughly 1-3%), but Pixel has grown fast in the United States, the United Kingdom, Japan, and Germany, and it dominates the developer and photographer segments. Every Pixel ships with a 15-digit IMEI (dual IMEIs on eSIM-capable models) tied to Google's authentication systems and, critically, to Factory Reset Protection (FRP).

Google Pixel resale is unusually risky compared with other Android brands. Because Pixel is sold direct through the Google Store and through only a handful of carriers, second-hand Pixel devices frequently move across borders — a Pixel 9 Pro bought unlocked in the US might turn up on a marketplace in Bangkok, Cairo, or Lagos within weeks. Verifying the IMEI is the fastest way to confirm the exact model, storage, and colour before you pay, and to catch handsets that are blocklisted or locked to a previous owner's Google account.

Our free Google IMEI lookup identifies your Pixel model instantly. Premium checks reveal deeper carrier and account status when you need certainty.

## How to Find Your Google Pixel IMEI

Four straightforward methods:

- **Dial `*#06#`** on the phone keypad — the IMEI (or both IMEIs, if the device supports dual SIM) appears immediately.
- **Settings → About phone → IMEI** — Pixels running Android 14/15 show IMEI 1, IMEI 2 (eSIM), and the serial in one screen.
- **Original retail box** — check the outer barcode label from the Google Store or your carrier.
- **Rear label under the SIM tray** — physical SIM Pixels engrave the IMEI on the metal tray or on a sticker beneath.

For Pixel Fold, Pixel Tablet with LTE, and Pixel Watch LTE, the IMEI lives in **Settings → About**.

## When Should You Run a Google Pixel IMEI Check?

- **Buying a US Pixel abroad.** Pixel is sold aggressively in the US market and often exported second-hand. Confirm the model matches the listing, and check for a carrier lock — a Verizon-locked Pixel will not activate on a foreign SIM.
- **Blacklist verification.** Google Pixel resale prices have made them a target for theft in some markets. Run a blacklist check before you pay cash on a marketplace deal.
- **FRP concern.** If the seller can't factory-reset the device in front of you, or if the setup process demands a Google login you don't have, the device is FRP-locked. A pre-purchase IMEI check flags devices that show recent Google account activity.
- **Warranty verification.** Preferred Care and standard Google warranty are transferable — but only if the device is genuine and unmodified. Run a warranty check before paying a premium for "still under warranty" claims.
- **Importing to a new country.** Bands vary by region — a Pixel 9 sold in Japan supports different sub-6 5G bands than the US model. The full device report reveals the exact model number (e.g., GNK6U vs G0DZQ) so you can cross-reference band support.
- **Counterfeit detection.** Fake Pixels are less common than fake iPhones or Xiaomis, but grey-market repair swaps do happen. A mismatched IMEI is an instant red flag.

## What to Do If Your Google Pixel IMEI Check Reveals a Problem

If the blacklist check comes back positive, treat the purchase as void — the device will not activate on any GSMA-connected carrier in most countries. Contact the seller for a refund and, if the transaction was in-person cash, report the encounter to local police so the buyer's chain of custody is documented in case the device surfaces again.

For a Factory Reset Protection flag, ask the seller to walk you through a full factory reset in your presence and sign into their Google account to remove FRP before you pay. If the setup wizard demands credentials you don't have, do not complete the purchase — a legitimate bypass requires the original account holder or a proof-of-purchase escalation to Google support, and there is no reliable third-party FRP removal tool for modern Pixels.

If the carrier lock check reveals a Verizon, T-Mobile, or Google Fi lock, verify that the seller has completed the required financing payoff and 60-day activation window before you buy. Otherwise you may end up waiting weeks for the carrier to process the unlock, during which the phone is unusable on your SIM.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does IMEI check work for Google Pixel phones sold in the US versus EU?**
A: Yes. IMEI is a global standard — the same 15-digit code works regardless of region. Our database identifies the exact regional variant so you know whether a Pixel 9 is the US, EU, or Japanese SKU (band support differs).

**Q: Can imeihub remove Factory Reset Protection from my Pixel?**
A: No. Imeihub verifies status only — we tell you whether FRP is likely active, but we don't remove or bypass it. Genuine FRP removal requires the original Google account credentials or proof-of-purchase escalation through Google support.

**Q: Are Google Pixel phones locked to specific carriers?**
A: Pixels bought unlocked from the Google Store are carrier-unlocked. Pixels financed through Verizon, T-Mobile, or Google Fi may be network-locked until the financing plan is paid off. Our carrier lock check confirms status in seconds.

**Q: I have two IMEIs on my Pixel — which one should I check?**
A: Either works, but they belong to different lines. IMEI 1 is typically the physical SIM slot, IMEI 2 is the eSIM. For blacklist and warranty checks, IMEI 1 is standard.

**Q: My Pixel says "no service" after a reset — is it blacklisted?**
A: Possibly, but there are other causes (SIM not activated, wrong APN, hardware fault). Run a blacklist check first — if the device is blocked on the GSMA registry, no SIM will work on any compatible carrier. See our [blacklist service page](/service.php?slug=blacklist) for the full workflow.

**Q: Does the free IMEI check show the Tensor chip generation?**
